Third party certification and testing

Read before relying on this matrix

  • Always read the BBA, Kiwa or UL certificate itself. The materials cited in the certificate must be used. Manufacturers are generally consistent, but third party suppliers often offer savings with a cheaper mortar or adhesive, and if that product is not on the certificate the cover is void.
  • Check certificate validity and the latest issue on the BBA, Kiwa or UL website before specifying. Certificates are withdrawn and reissued.
  • Check the certificate scope: substrate, building height, slip range and the support system it covers. A certificate for one product sheet does not cover the rest of a range.
  • Reaction to fire class is what the classification report or certificate states for the tested build-up. It does not transfer to a different insulation, membrane or subframe.

How to read the columns

  • Fix: M = mechanically fixed. A = adhesively fixed (slips bedded in adhesive or bonded to a board).
  • Not found: no certificate of that type was found in the manufacturer's published literature or on NBS Source at the check date. It does not prove none exists; ask the manufacturer.
  • Not checked: not yet looked up for this draft.
  • Weight: approximate installed weight. Projections and a denser brick will change it, though not by much.
  • Evidence status: Evidenced means a current UK third party system certificate was found. Partly evidenced means testing or a certificate exists but with a gap in scope or number. Manufacturer claims, not evidenced means performance claims without published third party certification.

Corrections and new evidence

Manufacturers and suppliers can submit current certificates, classification reports or test reports to neil@kirkbc.uk. Every submission is reviewed and the entry updated with the date of change. Entries change on evidence only, not on marketing literature.
